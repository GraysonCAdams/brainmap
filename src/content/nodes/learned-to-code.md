## Problem

There wasn't one. That's the honest version. Nobody set me a task; I had access to a computer and a father who worked with them, and the thing I wanted was to make something appear on a screen that hadn't been there before.

## Constraints

I was small enough that the constraint was comprehension. There was no course, no one to ask, and no framing for what any of it was for. Whatever I learned had to come from looking at something that already worked and changing it until it broke or improved.

## Approach

View source, essentially. Copy something, alter it, see what happens. That is a genuinely bad way to learn theory and a very good way to build the instinct that everything on a screen is made of something you can open.

The website came next. The earliest surviving capture of my own domain, from 2004, describes it in my own words at the time: *"This is my website as I complete first grade; the site began when I was in Kindergarten. It is here for you to enjoy and for me to learn some web design."*

## Edge cases considered

None, consciously. But the habits that formed here are the ones I still have, and I can date them.

By nine I had added scripts to stop people right-clicking my pages, with a note asking them not to take my content, which is a child's first collision with the fact that anything you serve you have already given away.

By twelve I was administering my own hosting, and the clearest artifact of that is a support ticket where I had changed a directory's permissions to 777, watched the folder vanish from view, and wrote in to say **"the folder entirely disappeared from view. Yet, it is still there."** I was wrong about the cause and completely right about the distinction. Something being unreachable and something being gone are different failures, and noticing that at twelve is more or less the whole job I do now.

## Tradeoffs

Learning by imitation left real gaps. I could build things well before I could explain why they worked, and it took years of professional work to fill in the theory underneath habits I already had.

The compensating advantage is that I have never once believed a system is a black box. If it renders, it's made of something, and that something can be opened.

## Outcome

Everything else on this map. The next node over is the domain itself, which then ran for eighteen more years.
