## Problem

Small businesses around Atlanta needed sites and could not evaluate the people selling them. Real estate offices, a musician, a food blogger, a couple of nonprofits. Most did not need anything technically interesting; they needed someone who would finish, and then still answer the phone.

## Constraints

I was in high school when this started, then in college, then employed full-time. Everything had to fit around that, which meant scope had to be nailed down before work began or it would eat weekends indefinitely.

## Approach

I ran it like a business rather than a series of favors. A published service price sheet with packaged tiers, a custom site at one price and a site with a blog and CMS at another, plus itemized upgrades priced individually. Signed contractor agreements, e-signed deliverables checklists, hosting and domain costs passed through at cost, and a written credential-handoff document at the end of each engagement.

## Edge cases considered

Two clauses appear verbatim in every contract from 2016 through 2021, and each exists because of something that happened once. Invoicing every five hours with "work will not continue until each payment is made" bounds my exposure on a client who stops paying midway. And "work product will become the property of the client after final payment, but until then is property of the contractor" makes the handoff moment unambiguous, which protects the client as much as it protects me.

For the largest engagement I put the client's own billing details on the hosting account rather than mine, so that if I disappeared they still owned the thing they had paid for. Same reasoning as the handoff document: the failure mode I was designing against was my own absence.

## Tradeoffs

My philosophy inverted completely over the decade, and I think it inverted correctly. An early résumé of mine brags that I "developed functional websites from ground up without aid of website platforms." By 2018 every site I delivered was WordPress on commodity hosting with a purchased theme.

Hand-coding produced better craft and worse outcomes. A client who cannot edit their own site calls me for a phone-number change forever, and a bespoke codebase becomes unmaintainable the moment I stop maintaining it. Buying a well-supported theme for around sixty dollars and spending the saved hours on content and structure served them better, even though it is less impressive to describe.

My rate went down rather than up over that period, from fifty-five dollars an hour to fifty for the largest and last big project. Predictability was worth more to me than the margin.

## Outcome

More than twenty clients across roughly twelve years, with documented paid engagements from 2016 through 2022 and pro-bono work for two nonprofits alongside them. Photoshop was the design tool from the first site to the last.

One correction worth recording: I had long remembered the real-estate contract as 2020. The signed audit trail puts it in **June 2018**. Checking beat remembering, which is most of why the archaeology behind this map was worth doing at all.
