## Problem

Not a problem so much as a place. For eighteen years this domain was wherever I was currently learning something in public, and it got torn down and rebuilt every time that changed.

## Constraints

Shared hosting, whatever tooling I had access to at each age, and no one to ask. The constraint that shaped it most was that I administered it myself from about age ten onward, so every capability I wanted required understanding the layer underneath it.

## Approach

Seven distinct sites on one domain. A hand-written kindergarten homepage. A Flash redesign. A self-hosted blog, photo gallery, and forums when it became an electronics and console fan site around age ten. Two eras of blogging. Then it stopped being a site and became a launchpad, redirecting to the products I was building. A freelance portfolio with a client portal. And finally, in 2020, a short-lived tech blog about the homelab.

## Edge cases considered

The most instructive failure is documented in a support ticket from April 2008, when I was twelve. In my own words at the time: "I was changing the CHMOD on the themes folder because it repeatedly said: Access denied. So I changed the CHMOD to 777 and the folder entirely disappeared from view. Yet, it is still there."

It had not disappeared. The permissions had ended up such that the web server could no longer traverse the directory, so the files existed and were unreachable. I was twelve, debugging Unix permissions on a server I was paying for with my parents' help, and had already worked out the crucial distinction: that "gone" and "inaccessible" are different failures with different fixes.

By 2005 I had also added scripts to block right-clicking, with a note asking people not to borrow my content. That is a nine-year-old's first encounter with the fact that anything you serve, you have already given away.

## Tradeoffs

Repeatedly wiping the site rather than maintaining it cost me continuity: there is no single archive of my own writing, and several eras survive only because the Internet Archive happened to be looking. If I could tell the twelve-year-old one thing it would be to migrate rather than reset.

The upside of resetting that often is that I have rebuilt the same thing on six different stacks, and I understand what each one actually bought.

## Outcome

The 2004 capture states the purpose plainly: "This is my website as I complete first grade; the site began when I was in Kindergarten. It is here for you to enjoy and for me to learn some web design."

The 2010 blog reached roughly seventy posts across fifteen pages. The last content era, in the summer of 2020, was about home networking, hypervisors, email privacy, and Home Assistant, which is to say I was already the person doing the work this site now documents. Since 2021 the domain has simply pointed at my LinkedIn.

This is the single best contemporaneous record of the whole arc, and it is why I trust the early dates on this map: they are not memory, they are captures.
